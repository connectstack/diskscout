## Title

**DiskScout — Know what's safe to delete on your Mac**

## Short description (Gumroad subtitle)

Find what's eating your Mac's storage and see, for every single item, whether removing it is safe. One click cleans the junk. Everything goes to the Trash first, with Undo.

## Long description

Every Mac fills up. Caches, old Xcode junk, leftovers from apps you deleted months ago, three copies of the same video. The hard part was never finding big files. The hard part is knowing which ones you can delete without breaking something.

DiskScout answers that question for every file and folder it shows you.

**Four safety levels, explained in plain English**

- **Safe** — caches and build products that macOS or the app rebuilds on its own.
- **Review** — your own files. Only you can decide, so DiskScout shows you a preview.
- **Caution** — app data and settings. Deleting works, but something may reset.
- **Protected** — system files and standard folders. DiskScout refuses to touch these.

Every rating comes with a one-line reason, and when something rebuilds itself DiskScout tells you the command that recreates it.

**Smart Clean**

One dashboard sizes every known junk location in seconds: app caches, browser caches, logs, Xcode DerivedData and old simulators, npm, Homebrew, pip, Gradle, Docker, installers you already ran, and leftovers from apps you've uninstalled. Click “Select everything safe” and you're done.

**See what's inside without opening forty folders**

Browse like Finder with a size bar on every row, or switch to the treemap for the whole picture at a glance. Show Media gathers every photo, video and audio file under a folder, however deeply nested, into one gallery with Quick Look previews.

**Duplicates, large files, old files**

Find exact duplicates and keep the one you choose. List the largest files anywhere. Find things you haven't opened in years.

**Clean your iPhone and iPad too**

Plug in an iPhone and it shows up in the sidebar. See what's filling it, how big every app is and how much each one has stored, then delete the apps you don't need. Browse the camera roll biggest-first (long videos are usually the culprit), look through the iPhone's folders and crash logs, and copy anything to your Mac before you delete it on the phone. Apps that may hold chats, authenticator codes, wallets or your documents are flagged Caution, and DiskScout points out apps that can free space themselves (WhatsApp, Telegram, Spotify…). It uses the same Apple framework Finder uses to sync, so there's nothing extra to install.

**Nothing on your Mac is ever deleted outright**

Every removal goes through a review sheet that lists each item with its reason. Items are moved to the Trash, never erased, and a single click puts a whole batch back. A history window remembers everything DiskScout moved. iPhones have no Trash, so deleting an app there is permanent, and the review sheet says so before anything happens.

**Private by design**

DiskScout never sends your file names or folder contents anywhere. The only network request it ever makes is to check a Gumroad license key when you paste one.

## Requirements

- macOS 14 Sonoma or later, Apple silicon or Intel.
- About 10 MB of disk space.

## Installing

1. Open the downloaded `DiskScout-2.0.0.dmg` and drag DiskScout into Applications.
2. Open DiskScout. The first time, macOS may ask to confirm you want to open an app from the internet.
3. Grant Full Disk Access when DiskScout asks. It walks you through the three steps. Without it, macOS hides Mail, Safari and other apps' data, and sizes will be too small.
4. Paste your license key under **DiskScout ▸ Activate License…** Gumroad buyers find the key in their receipt email and in the Gumroad library; Patreon supporters receive a key by message.
